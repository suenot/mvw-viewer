# Technique: build long work from short approved pieces

[Motion techniques](motion-techniques.md) · Reviewed November 16, 2026

The creator says outright that long pieces are easier to build as smaller sections ([10:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K1Mr5_7Gm3g&t=614s)). The tutorial on a code-drawn 2D video plugin shows the same habit from another angle: it renders a pilot of about 30 s before the full video, and the author then picks one scene of the pilot to change ([6:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YyUP_hSovOg&t=399s)).

```viz motion-techniques/assemble
caption: Four scenes of 5 s, each approved on its own, slide together into one 20 s strip. The three seams get a review flag for palette, audio and pacing.
```

## How it works

1. **Split the story into scenes** of a few seconds, each with one idea. The demo uses four scenes of 5 s.
2. **Build and approve each scene alone** against the [style contract](technique-style-contract.md), with its own [render and inspect](technique-render-and-inspect.md) pass. A defect is found in 5 s of film, not in 20.
3. **Join them** into one timeline. The scenes share the palette, the seed rules and the audio bed.
4. **Review the seams.** With four scenes there are three. Look at the last frame of one and the first of the next, and listen for a jump in level or ambience.

## Why it helps

- A correction touches one scene, so the others are not at risk.
- Scenes are reusable: an approved opening can be placed in the next film.
- A pilot gives early feedback. Render the first 8 to 10 s at draft quality and show it before the full build; after a render, send the owner a numbered sheet with one thumbnail per shot, so feedback can name a shot (see [Reference: 2D video plugin](reference-claude-2d-video-plugin.md)).

## Watch for

- **Seams.** Matching colour, grain and sound level at the cut is the work that remains. A shared contract prevents most of it; the review catches the rest.
- **Scenes that cannot stand alone.** If scene 3 needs scene 2 to make sense, approve them as a pair.
- **Per-scene seeds that reuse the same drawing.** Two scenes with one seed will boil in lockstep. See [Drawing cadence and a stable seed](technique-cadence-and-seed.md).
