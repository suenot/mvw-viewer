# Technique: three B-roll treatments and per-beat timing

[Motion techniques](motion-techniques.md) · Reviewed November 16, 2026

A talking-head video gets motion graphics in two decisions: what kind of layer each line gets, and when each element appears against the voice.

```viz motion-techniques/broll-treatments
caption: Top, a 26 second plan made of three treatments: A speaker, B overlay, C cover (A4 B5 A5 C5 A3 B2 A2 seconds, an example). Below, the timing choice for "revenue grew 30 percent": axes lead by about six frames, the value lands on the word, an answer waits.
```

## Three treatments

- **A, speaker.** The video alone. For the opening line, an opinion, an emotional beat, the call to action and the closing line.
- **B, overlay.** A transparent layer over the speaker: a keyword, a number counting up, an icon, a short list. Keep it toward the edges and out of the face.
- **C, cover.** An opaque full-frame scene hides the speaker while the voice continues. For numbers, comparisons, steps, an interface, anything with three or more parts.

Rhythm rules from the skill: open and close on A; no stretch of A longer than about 8 seconds; a C stretch runs about 3 to 8 seconds, with the speaker back between covers.

## Timing per element

| Timing | Use for |
| --- | --- |
| Lead by about six frames | Context the eye can prepare: axes, cards, process structure |
| Sync to the relevant word | New key facts: amounts, percentages, prices, final chart values |
| Wait for the reveal | Punchlines, negations, withheld answers, conclusions |

For "revenue grew 30%": bring in the chart structure early, then land the final bar value on the word "30%". Synchronization means the number is readable, not that a long count-up has started.
